Sound units and interactional units

Prosody includes pitch, prominence, rhythm, speed, and loudness.

An intonation unit is a stretch packaged by prosody.

It need not equal a TCU, a unit that can be treated as possibly complete in interaction.

In the bridge example, a sound boundary need not finish the instruction.

Gloss TCU provisionally here; teach its completion and turn-allocation implications through the later library sequence. Li (2014), pp. 83–104, 137–138, shows that intonation units and TCUs need not align in Mandarin. This bridge example is a teaching illustration, not one of Li’s extracts.

A transcript is not the data

The talk is the data. The transcript is a re-presentation of it.

It exists so that someone who was not there can still check what happened.

Every mark is a small decision about what matters in talk.

Why bother? Because claims need to be checkable

“He interrupted me.” · “She was annoyed.” · “We were more polite in English.”

A claim about talk should be checkable by someone else — against the record, line by line.

That is what makes analysis scientific instead of impressionistic.

A turn-constructional unit

A turn-constructional unit (TCU) is a stretch of talk participants can treat as possibly complete.

It can be a word, phrase, clause, or sentence.

“Upstairs” can complete an answer in this sequence.

Context and delivery matter—not grammar alone.

Finished enough—but still speaking?

A: The library is past the bridge. (0.3)
A: Then take the first left.

Where could B have spoken?

Did a possible completion disappear just because A continued?

A possible change is not an actual change

A transition relevance place (TRP) is a possible TCU completion where speaker change becomes relevant.

A may continue if no one takes the turn and no other speaker has been selected.

One turn can contain several TCUs and possible TRPs.

Relevant does not mean required.

Allow 60–90 seconds on the previous puzzle before revealing the term. Ground the model in the assigned chapter, pp. 54–56; Li (2014), pp. 5–8, applies it to Mandarin. Ask students to distinguish TCU boundaries, TRPs, and actual speaker changes.

How does the next speaker get in?

At a TRP:

  1. The current speaker selects the next one — a name, a question, a look.
  2. Nobody is selected, so someone self-selects.
  3. Nobody starts, so the current speaker continues.

Which case fits each library question? Where does the current speaker have the option to continue?

Timing is not enough

Overlap is simultaneous talk. It can acknowledge, anticipate a completion, or compete for the floor.

Starting before completion and pursuing the floor is a candidate interruption.

Examine timing, delivery, and both speakers’ next conduct. The label does not automatically tell us whether the action was rude.

Same word, different work?

A: Go past the bridge—
B: Yeah.
A: —then take the first left.
A: Is the bridge open?
B: Yeah.

Does “Yeah” do the same thing in both?

The “please continue” sounds

yeah · mhm · 嗯 · 对 · 哦

In an ongoing telling, a response may work as a continuer: acknowledging the talk without claiming an extended turn.

In a question–answer sequence, the same word may be an answer.

Identify the practice in context, not from a word list.

The sounds of silence

Where does the silence occur?

  • before someone else starts talking
  • after a question that clearly selects one speaker
  • inside one speaker’s own turn
  • nobody speaking at all

Then ask of each one: what is it doing?

Silence has no meaning of its own

The same (1.2) can be:

  • time to think of an answer,
  • time to check the map,
  • a repair being prepared,
  • a response being withheld.

Its meaning comes from where it sits in the sequence — the surrounding talk decides.
