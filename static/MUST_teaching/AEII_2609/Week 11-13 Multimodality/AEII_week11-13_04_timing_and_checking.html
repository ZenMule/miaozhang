When does the key turn?

A speaker explains how a lock works: lifting its internal pieces lets the key turn.

Which words go with the gesture?

McNeill (2005) separates two ideas:

Term Meaning
lexical affiliate the word(s) closest in meaning to the gesture — found by comparing gesture and word (Schegloff’s term)
co-expressive speech the speech that, in this context, expresses the same idea as the stroke — what the speaker is highlighting as significant

They may be the same words. They need not be.

One key, two comparisons

Return to the key-turning gesture:

  • Lexical affiliate: key to move — the gesture comes before it
  • Co-expressive speech: enables — the gesture is synchronous with it
  • The highlighted idea: lifting the tumblers makes turning possible

“Early” or “synchronous”? It depends on which words you measure against.

Two different questions

Question Evidence
temporal coordination when does the stroke/nucleus happen relative to the related speech? timestamps on independent tiers
semantic contribution what does the gesture add to the meaning? form + speech + context

A gesture can be perfectly timed and add nothing new. It can be early and still be crucial.

Keep the two answers separate in your evidence sheet.

What timing can and cannot show

  • It can show order and overlap of strokes and speech
  • It cannot show what the speaker was thinking
  • A lead or lag does not prove a cognitive process
  • There is no target percentage of synchrony

Defend one temporal observation without claiming causation or access to the speaker’s mind.

Checking each other

Annotate before you discuss

For the same short debate interval:

  1. Did we find the same events?
  2. Do the boundaries match?
  3. Do the labels match?
  4. Do the interpretations match?

Record disagreements — do not quietly erase them.

Have two students annotate the same interval independently before comparing. Use only recordings whose permission covers this access. If time is tight, check one matched event now and continue the comparison in the evidence clinic.

A classroom matching rule

Match events first. Then replay any boundary difference larger than two video frames.

Frame rate Two frames
25 fps 0.08 s
30 fps ≈ 0.067 s

Check the actual frame rate first; if unknown, report raw differences without pass/fail. This is a practice check, not a formal reliability study.

Three kinds of agreement

  • Boundary agreement: we cut the timeline in the same places
  • Label agreement: we gave the same phase / technique / dimension values
  • Interpretive agreement: we believe the gesture contributes the same thing

Agreement on one does not guarantee the others. Revise one codebook rule and one claim.

From tiers back to a transcript

A readable multimodal transcript

Tiers suit analysis; presentations and essays need a readable transcript. Mondada’s conventions extend the CA transcripts you know:

Symbol Meaning
* * / + + delimit one participant’s embodied action — one symbol per participant
.... action emerging (preparation)
---- action maintained
,,,, action withdrawing (retraction)
lower-case b the participant acting, if not the speaker
