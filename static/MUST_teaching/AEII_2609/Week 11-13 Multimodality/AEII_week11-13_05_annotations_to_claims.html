Tiers are not findings

Which one is a finding?

Claim 1

“The hand flicks twice. There are two gestures, so the speaker is very expressive.”

Claim 2

“In Kendon’s rice-scattering case, two wrist actions form one stroke within one gesture phrase. The scattering movement makes the general verb ‘throw’ more specific: it depicts spreading a powder, not throwing a single object.”

What evidence supports the second claim? Kendon (2004, pp. 114–116)

Both formulations are instructor-written; Claim 2 paraphrases Kendon’s actual analysis, while Claim 1 deliberately misreads it. Distinguish repeated action within a stroke from a count of gesture phrases. Neither permits a personality inference. The source’s written account supports the interpretation; making our own timing measurement would require access to the original recording.

The anatomy of a defensible claim

  • observable form, before labels
  • related speech, with justified word choice
  • timing and its uncertainty
  • context: surrounding turns, topic, and speakers
  • a bounded interpretation using the readings
  • a limitation or alternative reading

A finding connects them.

Two cases, not twenty

Look closely at your group-debate recording:

  • one case where you are confident
  • one case that is ambiguous or challenges your first idea

The second case often teaches you more than the first.

Aim for two cases from a short interval, extending the selection if needed rather than inventing an ambiguous case. Use the consented supplied debate data when recording was declined. The shared-video practice remains a method reference; do not pool its counts with debate-video counts or infer a controlled comparison.

Does uncertainty mean the analysis has failed?

M evokes a cellar through a downward action. His listener A replies that she has visited it once, while extending a finger forward.

Is A:

  • pointing toward the cellar space M has evoked?
  • marking one occasion, through the extended index finger?

Could both be relevant? Kendon (2004, pp. 164–165)

Paraphrase of Example 20. M’s pointing includes downward movement; A’s finger remains horizontal, so the movements are not identical. Kendon explicitly retains two non-exclusive interpretations of A’s action rather than choosing one. This is a return to the cheese-shop case, not a new required reading. Do not treat a classroom reenactment as the original data.

A stronger claim can retain an alternative

Kendon (2004, p. 165) retains both readings:

  • The preceding exchange supports a link to the cellar
  • The accompanying reference to once supports singularity
  • Neither interpretation rules the other out

Which features of the action and talk would your own claim need to show?

Model source-qualified phrasing: Kendon suggests that A may orient to the notional cellar, mark a unique visit, or do both. Do not convert this into evidence that A’s private intention is known. A useful limitation names the specific unresolved meaning, not just a generic need for more data.

Toward the final presentations

A structure you could use:

question → data & method (who, what task, which permissions) → replayable extract → observation → interpretation → contrasting or uncertain case → limitation

Not every group has to use ELAN. Every group has to show its evidence.

What we built

  • Speech alone under-specifies → look at the body
  • Labels are not explanations → describe first, then interpret
  • Gestures unfold in time → units, phrases, phases
  • Timing is a claim → independent tiers, exports, peer checks
  • Annotations are not findings → connect form, speech, timing, and context

Exit check

One claim. One interval that supports it. One limitation. One ELAN operation I can now do on my own.
